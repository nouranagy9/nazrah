## Guiding inquiry question

> How does the way Saudi/Gulf families care for non-verbal relatives at home
> differ from what commercial AAC (Augmentative and Alternative
> Communication) tools assume — and what does that mean for the phrase set,
> language, and interaction design of an eye-gaze communication board?

Questions worth asking:
- Who in the family is the primary caregiver day-to-day? Does that change
  through the day (e.g. at prayer times, at night)?
- What are the most common things the patient needs to communicate that
  are hard to get across right now?
- Are there phrases, terms of address, or routines (prayer, meals) that
  are specific to how *this* family does things, not generic?
- What's been tried before (gestures, letter boards, apps)? Why did it or
  didn't it work?
